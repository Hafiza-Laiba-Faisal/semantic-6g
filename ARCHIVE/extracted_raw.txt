D. DTCP: what it is--and is not

Literature-supported fact. DTCP means Delay-aware Trajectory-guided Control Prediction. It
was designed for Diao's delayed, edge-enabled autonomous-driving system, where camera data
become stale during uplink transmission, edge inference, downlink communication, and
control execution. [1]

DTCP combines two learned branches:

    A trajectory branch extracts trajectory features, autoregressively predicts future waypoint-
    related information, and uses a low-level PID controller to generate trajectory commands.
    A control branch predicts a sequence of future control commands using recurrent
    processing and information from the trajectory branch.
    A conditional combination rule fuses trajectory-branch and control-branch commands
    based on driving context, delay, and a threshold. [1]
The paper defines end-to-end delay as

[
\delta

\delta_e+\delta_u+\delta_a+\delta_d+\delta_c,
]

where encoding, uplink, agent computation, downlink, and control execution delays are
included. A prediction horizon is selected to compensate perceived delay, which is described
as    . [1]

Mathematical interpretation. DTCP is more than one-step action prediction. It predicts
sequences of future driving commands, and its trajectory branch predicts waypoint-related
outputs. It is learned and co-designed with communication, but its architecture, timing model,
CARLA vehicle interface, route representation, steering/throttle/brake command format,
turning condition, and PID details belong to the autonomous-driving problem.

Saturation

For evaluation, preserve the physical actuator constraint:

Hard clipping has a derivative of zero outside the unsaturated interval. This does not make
training impossible, but it can stall learning when the decoder repeatedly generates commands
beyond limits.

You have two defensible choices:

    Train and evaluate with hard clipping, while monitoring saturation frequency.
    Train with a smooth bounded actuator map such as

    then evaluate with the same smooth map.
The second choice changes the controller nonlinearity. Therefore:

        NOT SPECIFIED IN SOURCE -- PROJECT-SPECIFIC DECISION: hard clipping versus a
        smooth bounded control parameterization.

Do not train with and silently evaluate with a different hard-clipped plant; if you do
compare them, report it as an ablation.

CSI and fading

Your Rayleigh experiments need an explicit receiver assumption:

    Perfect CSI: receiver knows and equalizes. This is a clean initial benchmark.
    No CSI: decoder receives raw and must implicitly handle fading.
    Estimated CSI: receiver uses , introducing channel-estimation error.
Diao explicitly assumes perfect CSI in its channel equalization equation. That supports perfect
CSI as a benchmark assumption, but not as a claim of UAV realism.[1]

        NOT SPECIFIED IN SOURCE -- PROJECT-SPECIFIC DECISION: CSI acquisition, pilot
        overhead, estimation error, and whether CSI is appended to the decoder input.

Diao's thesis explicitly frames task-oriented communication as transmitting only information
needed for downstream machine decision-making rather than full data fidelity, and identifies
the autonomous-driving work as an application-specific communication/control co-design. The
thesis and 2025 paper support your conceptual direction, but neither fills the UAV-specific gaps
listed above. [6]

Things we must NOT assume from the literature

    Do not assume Diao's CARLA vehicle dynamics are valid for a UAV.

    Do not reuse CARLA steering, throttle, brake, waypoint, route, lane-following, collision, or
    driving-score definitions.

    Do not transfer Diao's trajectory branch, control GRU, image-attention blocks, or DTCP
    architecture into the low-dimensional UAV-state problem without separately justifying
    them.

    Do not treat Diao's delay threshold, delay decomposition, conditional branch-combination
    rule, or prediction horizon as UAV parameters.

    Do not claim Diao's action-posterior VIB loss is your UAV navigation loss.

    Do not claim that Diao specifies a double-integrator UAV controller.

    Do not invent , , , , , , or , or label any selected values "from Diao."

    Do not assume that decoding the full source state is required for task-oriented
    communication; Diao's architecture instead feeds received JSCC symbols to a task agent.

    Do not assume that direct control prediction is automatically fair against state-
    reconstruction baselines; it changes the control architecture unless carefully controlled.
   Do not assume that a terminal-only objective ensures safe, smooth, or low-energy
   navigation.
   Do not use binary success indicators as gradient objectives.
   Do not assume every-step transmission, communication period , packet hold behavior,
   latency, or age-of-information behavior from Diao's driving setup.
   Do not assume perfect CSI is realistic merely because it is used in Diao; state it as a
   benchmark assumption.
   Do not copy Diao's OFDM frequency-selective fading model into an i.i.d. flat-Rayleigh
   experiment without explicitly redefining the channel.
   Do not compare methods under different channel-use budgets, unequal SNR definitions,
   unequal power constraints, different controller gains, or different initial/goal distributions.
   Do not train the digital baseline end-to-end merely to make its training pipeline look
   similar to DeepJSCC; its role is a fixed algorithmic separated-communication comparator.
   Do not add a reconstruction MSE term to the primary task-oriented model by default. If
   included, it must be a separately motivated multi-objective ablation, because it partly
   changes the method back toward reconstruction-oriented communication.
   Do not present the recommended goal-relative representation or rollout loss as though
   they were copied from Diao. They are the proposed, mathematically coherent adaptation
   for this FYP.
