DeepJSCC Foundation: Bourtsoulatze et al. 2019 
https://www.perplexity.ai/search/9481c056-ba07-4402-ad09-6d23ec672738



The paper’s DeepJSCC core is a deterministic CNN autoencoder that maps a real-valued image vector directly to k complex channel uses, inserts a differentiable AWGN or slow-Rayleigh-fading channel layer, and reconstructs the image with a transpose-convolution decoder. For a low-dimensional UAV state, the channel equations, complex-symbol construction, power normalization, and definition k/n transfer directly; the image CNN and image-specific MSE/PSNR evaluation do not.[repository.essex.ac]
Source and notation
This extraction is from Bourtsoulatze, Kurka, and Gündüz, “Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding for Wireless Image Transmission,” IEEE TCCN, 2019—principally Sections II–IV and Figs. 1–6. The arXiv v4/preprint accessible here corresponds to the published paper’s content.[repository.essex.ac][arxiv]
A. Source representation
Explicitly stated — Section II, PDF p. 2; Section III, PDF p. 3
The source is an input image represented as a real vector:
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Variable definitions:
\mathbf{x}: input image, represented as an n-dimensional real-valued vector.
n: image dimension, called the source bandwidth by the paper.
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}: reconstructed image at the receiver.
For experiments, the source is an RGB image:
CIFAR-10: 32\times 32 RGB images, so n=32\times32\times3=3072.
Kodak evaluation: RGB images of 768\times512; training uses randomly cropped 128\times128 ImageNet patches.
Images are 24-bit RGB, i.e., 8 bits per colour channel.[repository.essex.ac]
Preprocessing
Explicitly stated — Section IV, PDF p. 4
Before the CNN encoder, image samples are normalized by the maximum pixel value, 255:
\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{\mathbf{x}}{255}.
This equation is an explicit algebraic restatement of the paper’s sentence, not a numbered equation in the paper.
Input values after normalization lie in [0,1].
At the decoder output, the final sigmoid produces values in [0,1].
A final denormalization layer multiplies by 255 to return pixel values to [0,255].
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER: a general normalization rule for arbitrary non-image sources, such as UAV position, velocity, heading, battery state, waypoint error, or sensor state. Min–max scaling, z-score standardization, clipping, and per-feature normalization for your UAV state are not provided by this paper.

Encoder and channel symbols
B. Encoder mapping
Explicitly stated — Section III, PDF p. 3
The encoder is a deterministic function
f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{k},
which maps the source image to channel inputs:
\mathbf{z}=f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{x}).
This mapping is stated in prose rather than presented as a separate numbered equation.
Variable definitions:
f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot): DeepJSCC joint source–channel encoder.
\boldsymbol{\theta}: trainable encoder-CNN parameters.
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}: real-valued input image.
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}: vector of k complex-valued channel-input samples.
k: channel dimension, called the channel bandwidth by the paper.
The encoder CNN consists of convolutional layers, PReLU activations, and a power-normalization layer. The paper says convolutional layers extract image features; nonlinear activations enable a nonlinear map from source space to coded-signal space.[repository.essex.ac]

where:

The paper explicitly states that the last encoder convolutional layer has 2k units in total. It varies the number of filters K in that last convolutional layer to obtain different values of k/n.[repository.essex.ac]
Important scope limitation: this is an image CNN with spatial convolutions and two downsampling stages. The paper does not specify a fully connected/MLP encoder for a low-dimensional vector state.
C. Real network outputs to complex symbols
Explicitly stated — Section IV, PDF p. 4
The last encoder convolutional layer produces 2k real-valued units. After power normalization, those 2k real values are combined into k complex-valued channel symbols.
In implementation terms, the paper explicitly establishes this structure:
\underbrace{(\text{real-valued neural output})}_{2k\text{ real units}}
\longrightarrow
\underbrace{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}}_{k\text{ complex channel samples}}.
The paper says the real and imaginary parts are directly transmitted over the in-phase and quadrature components of the digital signal.[repository.essex.ac]
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER: an explicit index-level pairing convention such as
z_i=a_i+j\,b_i
or whether the 2k output vector is ordered as all real parts followed by all imaginary parts, or interleaved (\Re z_1,\Im z_1,\Re z_2,\Im z_2,\ldots). You must choose and implement one consistent convention; do not claim that its ordering is specified by this paper.
D. Power normalization
Explicitly stated — Eq. (1), Section III, PDF p. 3
The required average-power constraint is
\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{z}^{*}\mathbf{z}\right]\leq P.
Variable definitions:
k: number of complex channel-input symbols.
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}: transmitted complex codeword/channel-input vector.
\mathbf{z}^{*}: conjugate transpose of \mathbf{z}.
\mathbf{z}^{*}\mathbf{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|z_i|^2: total energy of the complex codeword.
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]: expectation.
P: average transmit power per complex channel use.
The normalization rule is Eq. (1):
\boxed{
\mathbf{z}
=
\sqrt{kP}\,
\frac{\tilde{\mathbf{z}}}
{\sqrt{\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{*}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}}}
}
\tag{1}
where:
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}: encoder output before power normalization, as written in Section III.
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{*}: conjugate transpose of \tilde{\mathbf{z}}.
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}^{*}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\tilde z_i|^2: pre-normalization codeword energy.
P: target average transmit power per complex channel use.
\mathbf{z}: normalized transmitted codeword.
What Eq. (1) guarantees
From Eq. (1),
\mathbf{z}^{*}\mathbf{z}=kP,
for every nonzero encoder output \tilde{\mathbf z}. Thus each normalized codeword has energy kP, and therefore its power averaged over its k complex uses is P.
This conclusion is a direct algebraic consequence of Eq. (1), not an additional assumption.
Normalization location
Explicitly stated — Section IV, PDF p. 4
The power-normalization layer is located:
after the final encoder convolutional layer;
after its 2k real outputs have been produced;
before those values are combined into k complex channel symbols and passed through the channel.
Granularity
The paper’s Eq. (1) divides by the norm of the individual output vector \tilde{\mathbf z}. Therefore, the normalization is per encoded source sample / per codeword, not a batch-average operation.
Implementation caution: Eq. (1) contains division by \sqrt{\tilde{\mathbf z}^{*}\tilde{\mathbf z}}. Handling an exactly zero encoder output with an \epsilon denominator is a numerical implementation issue, but such an \epsilon is NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER.

Channel models and bandwidth
E. AWGN channel
Explicitly stated — Section III, PDF p. 4
The AWGN transfer function is
\eta_n(\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{n}.
Equivalently, the received vector in AWGN is
\hat{\mathbf z}=\mathbf z+\mathbf n.
This latter display is a direct substitution into the paper’s \hat{\mathbf z}=\eta(\mathbf z) notation; it is not separately numbered.
Variable definitions:
\eta_n:\mathbb C^k\rightarrow\mathbb C^k: AWGN-channel transfer function.
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb C^k: transmitted codeword.
\hat{\mathbf z}\in\mathbb C^k: received/noisy channel-output vector.
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb C^k: complex additive-noise vector.
\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf0,\sigma^2\mathbf I_k).
\mathcal{CN}: circularly symmetric complex Gaussian distribution.
\mathbf I_k: k\times k identity matrix.
\sigma^2: average noise power, as explicitly stated by the paper.
The entries of \mathbf n are i.i.d. circularly symmetric complex Gaussian samples.[repository.essex.ac]
Exact SNR definition
Explicitly stated — Eq. (5), Section IV, PDF p. 5
\boxed{
\mathrm{SNR}
=
10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P}{\sigma^2}\right)
\quad\text{dB}
}
\tag{5}
where:
P: average power of the coded channel-input signal after the encoder power-normalization layer.
\sigma^2: average complex-noise power.
\mathrm{SNR}: channel signal-to-noise ratio in dB.
The authors set P=1 in all reported experiments. They vary the SNR by varying \sigma^2.[repository.essex.ac]
Factor-of-two clarification
The paper specifies complex noise as
\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf0,\sigma^2\mathbf I_k)
and defines SNR using P/\sigma^2, not P/(2\sigma^2). Therefore:
\sigma^2 is the variance / average power of each complex noise sample in the paper’s notation.
If implementing the conventional circular complex Gaussian convention
n_i=n_{i,\mathrm R}+j n_{i,\mathrm I},
then the implementation must use
n_{i,\mathrm R},n_{i,\mathrm I}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2/2)
independently, so that \mathbb E|n_i|^2=\sigma^2.
The final real/imaginary component formula is a standard interpretation of the explicitly stated \mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2) convention; it is not written as an equation in the paper. The paper itself does not use a factor of 2 in Eq. (5).[repository.essex.ac]
F. Slow Rayleigh fading
Explicitly stated — Section III, PDF p. 4; Section IV-A, PDF pp. 6–7
The fading-only transfer function is
\eta_h(\mathbf z)=h\mathbf z,
where
h\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,H_c).
The composite slow-Rayleigh-plus-AWGN channel is
\boxed{
\eta(\mathbf z)
=
\eta_n(\eta_h(\mathbf z))
=
h\mathbf z+\mathbf n
}
where:
h: a scalar complex fading coefficient.
H_c: variance parameter of h, written by the paper as the covariance/scalar parameter in \mathcal{CN}(0,H_c).
\mathbf z\in\mathbb C^k: transmitted complex codeword.
\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma^2\mathbf I_k): AWGN.
\eta_h(\cdot): fading transformation.
\eta_n(\cdot): AWGN transformation.
\eta(\cdot): total fading-plus-noise channel transformation.
For its experiments, the paper sets
H_c=1,
and changes \sigma^2 to emulate different average SNRs.[repository.essex.ac]
Fading duration and CSI
Explicitly stated — Section IV-A, PDF pp. 6–7
This is block/slow fading at the image-codeword level:
A single h is sampled for each transmitted image.
The same h stays constant for all k channel symbols used to transmit that image.
A new independently sampled h is used for the next image.
The paper assumes:
No CSI at the transmitter.
No CSI at the receiver.
No pilot transmission.
No explicit channel equalization.
The authors interpret the trained network’s behavior as suggesting that it learns to estimate/adapt to the channel state through training, but they do not add an explicit estimator or an equalizer block.[repository.essex.ac]
For the fading experiments, the paper refers to average SNR as
\mathrm{SNR}
=
10\log_{10}
\left(
\frac{\mathbb E[h^2]P}{\sigma^2}
\right).
This expression appears in the paper’s fading-evaluation discussion. Note that the paper writes \mathbb E[h^2], although for a complex fading coefficient the usual physical average channel power would conventionally be expressed using \mathbb E[|h|^2]. Do not silently rewrite the paper’s printed expression as a different equation. With the experiment setting H_c=1, the intended normalized average fading power is one. [repository.essex.ac]
G. Bandwidth compression ratio
Explicitly stated — Section II, PDF p. 2
The paper defines:
n: source bandwidth, i.e., the real dimension of the source image vector \mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n.
k: channel bandwidth, i.e., the number of complex-valued channel-input samples \mathbf z\in\mathbb C^k.
Its bandwidth-compression ratio is:
\boxed{
\frac{k}{n}
}
This is stated in prose and is not assigned an equation number in the paper.
This is crucial:
Quantity
Paper’s convention
Source dimension n
Number of real source dimensions
Channel dimension k
Number of complex channel uses
Neural output immediately before complex packing
2k real values
Reported bandwidth ratio
k/n, not 2k/n
Typical compression regime
k<n

Thus, for your UAV state \mathbf s\in\mathbb R^{n_{\text{UAV}}}, if you use the paper’s convention and transmit k complex symbols, the directly corresponding ratio is:
\frac{k}{n_{\text{UAV}}}.
That is a direct transfer of the paper’s dimension convention. It does not mean the latent neural tensor has only k real degrees of freedom: it has 2k real components that are grouped into k complex channel uses.
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER: whether a low-dimensional UAV navigation state should use a compression regime k<n_{\text{UAV}}, equality k=n_{\text{UAV}}, or expansion k>n_{\text{UAV}}. The original paper studies images and generally discusses k<n.

Decoder, optimization, and tests
H. Decoder
Explicitly stated — Section III, PDF p. 4; Section IV, PDF p. 4
The decoder is
g_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}:\mathbb C^k\rightarrow\mathbb R^n
and computes
\hat{\mathbf x}=g_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf z}),
\qquad
\hat{\mathbf z}=\eta(\mathbf z).
Variable definitions:
g_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\cdot): joint source–channel decoder.
\boldsymbol{\phi}: trainable decoder-CNN parameters.
\hat{\mathbf z}\in\mathbb C^k: channel output after noise and, where applicable, fading.
\hat{\mathbf x}\in\mathbb R^n: reconstructed image.
The decoder first converts the real and imaginary components of the k received complex samples back into 2k real values, then processes them through transpose-convolution layers.

NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER: a decoder architecture for a generic vector source such as a UAV state. In particular, it does not prescribe an MLP width, number of hidden layers, activation function, residual connection, state constraint, output clipping rule, or state-space decoder.
I. Training
Explicitly stated — Eqs. (2)–(3), Section III/IV, PDF pp. 4–5
The general end-to-end objective is Eq. (2):
\boxed{
(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*},\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*})
=
\underset{\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi}}{\arg\min}\;
\mathbb E_{p(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x})}
\left[
d(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x})
\right]
}
\tag{2}
Variable definitions:
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}: optimized encoder parameters.
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*}: optimized decoder parameters.
p(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x}): joint distribution of original and reconstructed source samples.
d(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x}): chosen distortion measure.
\mathbb E_{p(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x})}[\cdot]: expected distortion over the joint source/reconstruction distribution.
For its image experiments, the paper uses MSE. Its finite-sample training loss is Eq. (3):
\boxed{
L
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
d(\mathbf x_i,\hat{\mathbf x}_i)
}
\tag{3}
with
d(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x})
=
\frac{1}{n}
\left\|
\mathbf x-\hat{\mathbf x}
\right\|^2.
Variable definitions:
L: batch/sample-average training loss.
N: number of samples in the loss average; in minibatch training, this is the minibatch size.
\mathbf x_i: i-th original image.
\hat{\mathbf x}_i: reconstruction of the i-th image.
d(\mathbf x_i,\hat{\mathbf x}_i): MSE distortion for sample i.
n: number of real source dimensions/pixel values.
\|\cdot\|: Euclidean norm.
The optimizer is Adam. The implementation is TensorFlow, not PyTorch.[repository.essex.ac]

Training SNR strategy
The paper does not train one model jointly across a distribution of SNR values. Instead:
Each DeepJSCC model/curve is trained for a specific target channel SNR, \mathrm{SNR}_{\text{train}}.
The model is evaluated at potentially different \mathrm{SNR}_{\text{test}}.
For figures that compare bandwidth ratios at a fixed SNR, the same SNR is used for training and evaluation.
This distinction is explicit in the discussion of Figs. 3–6.[repository.essex.ac]
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER:
joint multi-SNR training;
random SNR sampling per minibatch;
curriculum training over SNR;
exact random seed;
Adam \beta_1,\beta_2,\epsilon, weight decay, gradient clipping, initializer, or learning-rate schedule beyond the reported CIFAR change;
exact epoch count / exact iteration count at convergence;
a PyTorch implementation.
J. Testing and evaluation
Explicitly stated — Section IV, PDF pp. 5–8
The primary metric is PSNR:
\boxed{
\mathrm{PSNR}
=
10\log_{10}
\left(
\frac{\mathrm{MAX}^{2}}{\mathrm{MSE}}
\right)
\quad \text{dB}
}
\tag{4}
Variable definitions:
\mathrm{MAX}: maximum allowable pixel value.
\mathrm{MSE}=d(\mathbf x,\hat{\mathbf x}): image MSE.
For their RGB image experiments, \mathrm{MAX}=2^8-1=255.
They state that PSNR quantifies all reported performance, while also discussing SSIM as a perceptual-quality measure.[repository.essex.ac]

For CIFAR-10, the paper evaluates bandwidth ratios including k/n=1/12, 1/6, and, for fading robustness, 1/3. It plots a wider sweep of k/n in the bandwidth-ratio figures.[repository.essex.ac]
The image-specific digital comparison comprises JPEG/JPEG2000 and, for Kodak experiments, practical LDPC codes with BPSK, 4-QAM, 16-QAM, and 64-QAM. That comparison is outside the core DeepJSCC foundation and should not be treated as a required component of your UAV implementation.[repository.essex.ac]

\mathbf s\in\mathbb R^{n_{\mathrm{UAV}}}
\;\xrightarrow{\,f_{\boldsymbol\theta}\,}\;
\tilde{\mathbf z}
\;\xrightarrow{\text{Eq. (1)}}\;
\mathbf z\in\mathbb C^k
\;\xrightarrow{\,\eta\,}\;
\hat{\mathbf z}\in\mathbb C^k

This report separates what is directly stated in original DeepJSCC/task-oriented papers from standard complex-baseband mathematics and from choices that remain open for your UAV-navigation FYP. The most important conclusion is that the seminal DeepJSCC work defines a complex channel-vector model and a per-codeword normalization mathematically, but it does not specify PyTorch complex-tensor usage, a PyTorch tensor layout, or a universal Rayleigh equalization convention. Its physical-layer conventions can inform your later specification, but its source model, loss, architecture, channel-use ratio, fading/CSI assumptions, and SNR protocol are not automatically mandated for an FYP whose source is s_t\in\mathbb{R}^{k_s}.
Verified mathematical conventions
A–B. Complex representation and real-to-complex mapping
The original DeepJSCC formulation defines an encoder
f_\theta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{k},
\qquad
\mathbf z=f_\theta(\mathbf x)\in\mathbb{C}^{k},
where n is called the source bandwidth and k the channel bandwidth. The channel output is likewise complex, \hat{\mathbf z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}. The bandwidth ratio used conceptually is therefore k/n: complex channel uses per real source dimension.[discovery.ucl.ac]
The ICASSP implementation description associated with the original method then makes the real-valued neural implementation explicit:
The encoder FC layer output has size 2k.
The decoder FC input has size 2k.
The paper says the system transmits the real and imaginary parts through the in-phase and quadrature components.
Therefore, in that reported CNN implementation, k complex channel symbols are represented by 2k real scalar outputs/inputs.[discovery.ucl.ac]
The paper does not write a numbered equation spelling out the grouping convention, such as
z_i=a_i+j b_i,
nor does it state whether the 2k-vector ordering is interleaved,
[a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,\ldots,a_k,b_k],
or stacked,
[a_1,\ldots,a_k,b_1,\ldots,b_k].
Therefore:
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: the real/imaginary ordering of the 2k FC outputs.
The source also says the implementation was in TensorFlow, not PyTorch. It does not say that TensorFlow complex tensors were used, nor does it provide a tensor-level implementation.[discovery.ucl.ac]
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: whether the original DeepJSCC implementation used native complex tensors internally.
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: whether a PyTorch implementation should use torch.complex64/torch.complex128 or two real channels.

C–D. Transmit-power normalization
The original DeepJSCC encoder produces an unnormalized vector \tilde{\mathbf z}\in\mathbb C^k, followed by the normalization layer
\mathbf z
=
\sqrt{kP}\,
\frac{\tilde{\mathbf z}}
{\sqrt{\tilde{\mathbf z}^{*}\tilde{\mathbf z}}},
\tag{DeepJSCC Eq. 1}
where (\cdot)^* is conjugate transpose. This is the exact form reported in the ICASSP precursor of the TCCN paper.[discovery.ucl.ac]
The stated average-power constraint is
\frac{1}{k}\mathbb E[\mathbf z^{*}\mathbf z]\leq P.
Under the displayed normalization, for every nonzero output codeword,
\frac{1}{k}\mathbf z^{*}\mathbf z=P.
That is stronger than merely satisfying the expectation constraint: it fixes the average energy over the k symbols in each encoded codeword to P.

With P=1, Eq. (1) gives

for each nonzero codeword. This is not the same as requiring each complex symbol to have |z_i|^2=1. Individual symbols can have unequal energies.

\mathbf z_{\mathrm{in}}
=

\tag{ATROC Eq. 22}
That is a valid paper-defined convention, but it is not the same pipeline as unquantized original DeepJSCC.[pure.manchester.ac]
E–F. AWGN, complex-noise variance, and SNR
For a complex AWGN channel, the standard model is
\mathbf y=\mathbf z+\mathbf n,
\qquad
\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma_n^2\mathbf I).
Under the standard circularly symmetric complex Gaussian definition,
n_i=n_{i,R}+j n_{i,I},
with
n_{i,R}, n_{i,I}
\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}
\mathcal N\left(0,\frac{\sigma_n^2}{2}\right).
Hence,
\mathbb E[|n_i|^2]
=
\mathbb E[n_{i,R}^2]+\mathbb E[n_{i,I}^2]
=
\sigma_n^2.
Tse and Viswanath explicitly define a standard complex Gaussian w\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1) as having real and imaginary components each of variance 1/2, and use complex AWGN w\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N_0).[web.stanford]
Therefore, if a source writes
n\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2),
then the unambiguous interpretation under this convention is:
\operatorname{Var}(n_R)=\operatorname{Var}(n_I)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2},
\qquad
\operatorname{Std}(n_R)=\operatorname{Std}(n_I)
=
\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{2}}.
The original DeepJSCC precursor writes its slow-Rayleigh noise as
\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N_0\mathbf I_k)
and varies N_0 while setting P=1. It does not provide an explicit equation labeled “\mathrm{SNR}=P/N_0” in the extractable text, but this is the internally consistent complex-baseband interpretation: per-complex-symbol signal power P divided by per-complex-symbol noise power N_0.[discovery.ucl.ac]
Under that convention,
\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{linear}}
=
\frac{P}{\sigma_n^2},
\qquad
\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{dB}}
=
10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P}{\sigma_n^2}\right).
If P=1,
\sigma_n^2
=
10^{-\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{dB}}/10}.
Then each real noise coordinate must have standard deviation
\sigma_{\mathrm{real}}
=
\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_n^2}{2}}
=
\sqrt{\frac{10^{-\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{dB}}/10}}{2}}.
This “divide variance by two before generating I/Q noise” is required only if \sigma_n^2 was defined as the total variance/power of one complex sample.

G. E_s/N_0, E_b/N_0, and DeepJSCC SNR
For a conventional coded digital system, if a complex modulation symbol carries m=\log_2M coded bits and the channel-code rate is R_c information bits per coded bit, then
E_s = (R_c m)E_b,
so, when the same N_0 convention is used,
\frac{E_s}{N_0}
=
R_c \log_2(M)\frac{E_b}{N_0}.
In dB,
\left(\frac{E_s}{N_0}\right)_{\mathrm{dB}}
=
\left(\frac{E_b}{N_0}\right)_{\mathrm{dB}}
+
10\log_{10}\!\big(R_c\log_2 M\big).
DeepJSCC does not produce an explicit bit stream, does not choose a QAM order in the original analog-style method, and does not have a conventional channel-code rate. Therefore:
E_b/N_0 is not inherently defined for original unquantized DeepJSCC.
The natural DeepJSCC quantity is symbol-power SNR, P/\sigma_n^2, or equivalently E_s/N_0 when E_s=P per complex use.
A fair comparison against a digital baseline must align the same physical resources: complex channel uses, average transmit energy, noise convention, bandwidth ratio, block duration, and CSI knowledge.
Converting only the dB labels without matching those resources is not sufficient.
The original DeepJSCC comparison uses a capacity-based digital reference whose rate depends on channel bandwidth and channel capacity; it is not a claim that DeepJSCC uses E_b/N_0.[discovery.ucl.ac]
Fading, CSI, decoder, and gradients
H–I. Original DeepJSCC slow-Rayleigh model
For its slow-Rayleigh experiment, the original DeepJSCC work specifies
\hat{\mathbf z}
=
\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf h)\mathbf z+\mathbf n,
with
\mathbf h\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,H_c\mathbf I_k),
\qquad
\mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N_0\mathbf I_k),
and sets
H_c=1,\qquad P=1.
It further states that the channel gain is sampled from a Rayleigh distribution, remains constant during transmission of the whole image, and changes independently for the next image.[discovery.ucl.ac]
There is a notation tension worth preserving:
The displayed vector equation writes \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf h)\mathbf z, which superficially permits an elementwise fading vector.
The prose explicitly says the channel gain remains constant for the whole image, which corresponds to a scalar h repeated over all k channel symbols:
y_i=h z_i+n_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,k.
The prose gives the physical fading-coherence interpretation. The paper does not explicitly write \mathbf h=h\mathbf 1.
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: an explicit reconciliation of the vector notation \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf h)\mathbf z with the prose statement “constant for the whole image.”
For a standard scalar complex fading coefficient
h\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1),
complex-Gaussian theory implies
h=h_R+jh_I,
\qquad
h_R,h_I\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1/2),
and
\mathbb E[|h|^2]=1.
Its magnitude |h| is Rayleigh distributed and its phase is uniform. [web.stanford]
The later task-oriented TCS-JSCC source states this model explicitly: h\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1), Rayleigh amplitude, uniform phase, P=1, and a fading coefficient fixed per image but independently varying across transmissions. It defines
\mathrm{SNR}
=
10\log_{10}
\left(
\frac{\mathbb E[|h|^2]P}{\sigma_n^2}
\right).
\tag{TCS-JSCC Sec. II}
This is consistent with unit-mean fading power.[repository.essex.ac]
J–K. CSI and equalization
The original DeepJSCC slow-Rayleigh experiment explicitly assumes:
No CSI at transmitter.
No CSI at receiver.
No explicit pilot transmission.
No channel estimation.
No explicit equalizer such as \tilde y=y/h.
The decoder receives the faded/noisy channel output and is trained end-to-end to recover the source from it.[discovery.ucl.ac]
Thus the original SISO slow-Rayleigh processing order is:
\mathbf z
\rightarrow
h\mathbf z
\rightarrow
h\mathbf z+\mathbf n
\rightarrow
g_\phi(\hat{\mathbf z}).
There is no paper-supported division by h in that original setup.
By contrast, the task-oriented ATROC source assumes full receiver CSI for its modulation chain and uses zero-forcing equalization:
\check{\mathbf z}
=
\frac{h^*}{|h|^2}\mathbf z_{\mathrm{out}},
\tag{ATROC Eq. 23}
followed by an additional scaling operation before nearest-constellation detection.[pure.manchester.ac]
The DeepJSCC-MIMO paper is a different physical-layer setting: it has receiver-side CSI in open-loop MIMO and applies explicit ZF plus a learned residual correction before the decoder; in its closed-loop case it uses SVD-based operations/equalization.[arxiv]
These sources are not interchangeable.

L. Decoder input representation
Mathematically, original DeepJSCC says the decoder is
g_\phi:\mathbb C^k\rightarrow\mathbb R^n.
Its reported CNN architecture says the noisy channel samples enter an FC layer of input size 2k in the complex case. That supports a real 2k-coordinate decoder input representation.[discovery.ucl.ac]
However:
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: batch tensor shape.

NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: whether channel symbols were stored as a final tensor axis of size two, as two feature channels, or as a flattened FC vector.
A later DeepJSCC-derived paper makes one possible convention explicit: concatenate \Re(\mathbf y) and \Im(\mathbf y) into a real 2k-vector before decoding. That supports this as a common implementation pattern, but it does not retroactively establish it as the original paper’s specified ordering.[arxiv]
M–N. Differentiability and backpropagation
The original DeepJSCC paper explicitly requires the channel transfer function to be differentiable so gradients can be computed and errors backpropagated. It models the channel as a non-trainable layer inside the autoencoder.[discovery.ucl.ac]
For a sampled AWGN realization,
\mathbf y=\mathbf z+\mathbf n,
the derivative with respect to the transmitted vector is
\frac{\partial \mathbf y}{\partial\mathbf z}=\mathbf I.
For a sampled fading realization,
\mathbf y=h\mathbf z+\mathbf n,
the derivative is
\frac{\partial\mathbf y}{\partial\mathbf z}=h\mathbf I
for scalar block fading, or \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf h) for componentwise fading.
The noise and fading draws are exogenous random values in each forward pass; standard automatic differentiation propagates gradients through the realized arithmetic with respect to \mathbf z. The gradients do not need to differentiate “through the random-number generator” to train the encoder/decoder under this channel model.
The original DeepJSCC source establishes differentiable channel insertion, but it does not provide a low-level autodiff statement such as “detach noise” or “retain gradients through h.”
NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER/SOURCE: an exact framework-level rule for requires_grad, detach, random-number seeding, or gradient handling of sampled channel variables.
The power-normalization map in DeepJSCC Eq. (1) consists of norm, square root, division, and multiplication. It is differentiable for \tilde{\mathbf z}\ne0, so it can remain in the computational graph. That mathematical conclusion is consistent with the paper’s end-to-end differentiable architecture, though it does not publish autodiff source code.[discovery.ucl.ac]

Parameters That Must NOT Be Assumed Before Final Specification
Complex tensor representation: native PyTorch complex dtype versus real-valued tensors carrying I/Q components is not specified by the original DeepJSCC paper.
I/Q packing order: stacked [\Re(\mathbf y),\Im(\mathbf y)], alternating I/Q coordinates, separate tensor axis, or separate feature channels is not specified by the original paper.
Source-to-channel bandwidth ratio: the original paper’s k/n definition is for image dimensions and complex channel uses; your UAV-state definition must be formally declared.
Meaning of one channel use: one complex I/Q symbol versus one real scalar dimension must be fixed before reporting bandwidth efficiency.
Channel type for each experiment: real AWGN and complex AWGN have different dimensions and different noise-variance handling in the original DeepJSCC study.
Noise-variance convention: whether a stated \sigma^2 means total complex noise power or variance of each real coordinate must be declared. Do not apply the factor 1/2 twice or omit it.
SNR definition: pre-fading P/\sigma_n^2, average faded \mathbb E|h|^2P/\sigma_n^2, instantaneous |h|^2P/\sigma_n^2, and post-equalization SNR are different quantities.
Rayleigh fading coherence: original DeepJSCC’s narrative uses one coefficient for an entire image; a UAV-state experiment must specify one coefficient per state, per trajectory segment, per packet, or per symbol.
Rayleigh notation reconciliation: if adopting the original paper, clarify whether \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf h) means a repeated scalar block fade or genuinely elementwise fading.
CSI assumptions: no CSIT/no CSIR, CSIR only, imperfect CSIR, or CSIT/CSIR lead to different valid models and cannot be mixed.
Equalization: original DeepJSCC does not equalize its slow-Rayleigh signal; ATROC and MIMO papers do. Do not insert y/h, ZF, MMSE, or channel inversion without specifying the corresponding CSI model.
Order of receiver operations: faded/noisy signal directly into decoder versus equalize then decode versus pass CSI to decoder must be frozen.
Power-normalization location: continuous encoder output before channel, or quantized constellation symbols after quantization, are distinct paper-defined pipelines.
Power-normalization granularity: per codeword is supported by original DeepJSCC Eq. (1); batch-level and dataset-level alternatives are not supported by that equation.
SNR training distribution: fixed SNR-specific, curriculum, uniform random per batch, or uniform random per sample are different protocols found across papers.
Evaluation protocol: matched train/test SNR, SNR sweep, unseen-SNR generalization, and fading realization averaging must be explicitly specified.
Whether the UAV receiver’s task loss is trained through the physical channel: task-oriented papers differ in architecture and objective. The navigation loss, state estimator, and control-policy interface require a project-level specification rather than an inferred DeepJSCC default.
