Shao, Mao, and Zhang (2022), the paper on task-oriented communication for edge inference using the Information Bottleneck framework. 
https://www.perplexity.ai/search/762bc4e3-b85f-448a-8679-83c1089d3116


Shao, Mao, and Zhang’s paper formulates device–edge inference, not UAV navigation: a device maps an input sample to channel symbols, an AWGN channel corrupts them, and an edge server directly predicts a task label from the corrupted received representation. Its reusable contribution for your FYP is the task-aware, differentiable IB/VIB training principle; it does not supply a UAV state model, navigation task, controller, or trajectory/control loss.[arxiv]
Scope and notation
Primary source used: J. Shao, Y. Mao, and J. Zhang, “Learning Task-Oriented Communication for Edge Inference: An Information Bottleneck Approach,” IEEE JSAC, vol. 40, no. 1, pp. 197–211, Jan. 2022. The source text below follows the authors’ arXiv version (v3, Jan. 2023), which contains the paper’s equations and section numbering. The paper evaluates image-classification tasks, principally MNIST and CIFAR-10; it is not a UAV-navigation paper.[arxiv][github]
The paper’s notation convention is:
Upper-case letters, e.g. X,Y,Z,\hat Z,\hat Y, are random variables.
Lower-case letters, e.g. \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z},\hat{\mathbf{z}},\hat{\mathbf{y}}, are realizations.
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q) is KL divergence.
\mathbb{E}[\cdot] is expectation.
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) is a Gaussian distribution with mean \boldsymbol{\mu} and covariance \boldsymbol{\Sigma}.
\mathbf{I} is the identity matrix.[arxiv]
A. Problem formulation
What the paper explicitly states — Section II-A, p. 3
The setting is a device–edge co-inference system. A resource-constrained mobile/edge device runs an on-device DNN; an edge server runs a second DNN. The device transmits an extracted encoded feature over a wireless channel, and the server performs the final inference. Examples given by the paper are image classification and object detection.[arxiv]

The complete probabilistic pipeline is Equation (1):
Y \rightarrow X \rightarrow Z \rightarrow \hat Z \rightarrow \hat Y .
\tag{1}
The associated conditional factorization is stated as:
p(\hat{\mathbf{y}},\hat{\mathbf{z}},\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})
=
p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\,p_{\mathrm{channel}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{z})
\,p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}).
\tag{1, stated factorization}
Definitions:
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}): on-device feature-extraction plus JSCC-encoding conditional distribution.
p_{\mathrm{channel}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{z}): wireless-channel conditional distribution.
p_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}): server-side inference conditional distribution.
The authors explicitly say that the feature extractor first identifies task-relevant information from \mathbf{x}, and the JSCC encoder maps it to channel symbols \mathbf{z}. The server then uses \hat{\mathbf{z}} directly to infer \hat{\mathbf{y}}, without reconstructing \mathbf{x}.[arxiv]
Interpretation for your project: Equation (1) is a general communication-to-task Markov-chain template. You may reuse its structural pattern only after defining a UAV-specific source/state and task variable. The paper does not establish that an image label Y can be replaced without modification by a UAV action, waypoint, future state, or navigation success variable.

Information Bottleneck objective
B. Exact task-oriented objective
What the paper explicitly states — Section II-B, pp. 3–4
The original Information Bottleneck objective is Equation (2):
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IB}}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
=
-\underbrace{I(\hat Z,Y)}_{\text{Distortion}}
+
\beta\underbrace{I(\hat Z,X)}_{\text{Rate}}.
\tag{2a}
Here:
I(\hat Z,Y): mutual information between the received noise-corrupted representation and target Y.
I(\hat Z,X): mutual information between the received representation and original input.
\beta>0: trade-off coefficient.
\boldsymbol{\phi}: device-network parameters.
\hat Z, not Z, is used in both information terms. Therefore the objective explicitly accounts for channel corruption.
The paper rewrites Equation (2) as:
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IB}}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
&=
\mathbb{E}_{p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}
\Bigg[
\mathbb{E}_{p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})}
\big[-\log p(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})\big]
\\
&\qquad\qquad+
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\big(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})
\|p(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\big)
\Bigg]
-H(Y)
\\
&\equiv
\mathbb{E}_{p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}
\Bigg[
\mathbb{E}_{p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})}
\big[-\log p(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})\big]
+
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\big(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})
\|p(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\big)
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
The final equivalence drops H(Y), because the authors treat it as constant for optimization.[arxiv]
The paper describes the terms as follows:
The first term -I(\hat Z,Y) is called distortion.
Since H(Y) is fixed by the data distribution, minimizing -I(\hat Z,Y) is equivalent to minimizing H(Y\mid\hat Z).
The second term I(\hat Z,X) is called rate and interpreted as information preserved in \hat Z about X, measured through minimum description length.
Increasing \beta increases the relative pressure to compress/remove input information; it is the rate–distortion trade-off parameter.[arxiv]
C. Exact VIB reformulation
What is compressed: information about raw input X retained in the received feature \hat Z, measured by I(\hat Z,X).
What is preserved: information in \hat Z useful for the task target Y, measured by I(\hat Z,Y).
The paper says that the distributions p(\hat{\mathbf{z}}) and p(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}) are intractable for high-dimensional data. It introduces:
q(\hat{\mathbf{z}}): variational approximation/prior for p(\hat{\mathbf{z}}).
q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}): variational task-prediction distribution approximating p(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}).
\boldsymbol{\theta}: parameters of the server-side inference network.[arxiv]
The paper’s VIB objective is Equation (3):
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VIB}}(\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\mathbb{E}_{p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}
\left[
\mathbb{E}_{p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})}
\left[-\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})\right]
+
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})
\|q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\right)
\right].
\tag{3}
The authors call Equation (3) a tractable upper bound on the IB objective in Equation (2). Their stated mechanism is:
Replace the unavailable posterior p(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}) with q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}).
Replace the unavailable marginal p(\hat{\mathbf{z}}) with q(\hat{\mathbf{z}}).
Optimize the resulting expected negative log-likelihood plus weighted KL regularizer.[arxiv]
Important boundary: The main text explicitly says that the details of the upper-bound derivation are deferred to Appendix A. The paper’s main-text equations provide the original IB form, Equation (2), and final trainable VIB form, Equation (3). Do not represent a further algebraic derivation as a new paper equation unless you reproduce it from that appendix exactly.
Monte-Carlo trainable objective
For minibatch data \{(\mathbf{x}_m,\mathbf{y}_m)\}_{m=1}^{M}, with L channel-noise samples per data point, the empirical objective is Equation (4):
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VIB}}(\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
\simeq
\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\Bigg[
\frac{1}{L}
\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\Big[
-\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\big(
\mathbf{y}_m\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{m,l}
\big)
\Big]
+
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\Big(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x}_m)
\|q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\Big)
\Bigg],
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
where
\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{m,l}=\mathbf{z}_m+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{m,l},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{m,l}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^2\mathbf{I}).
Definitions:
M: minibatch size.
L: number of independently sampled channel-noise realizations for each training example.
m: data-sample index.
l: noise-sample index.
\mathbf{z}_m: transmitted representation for sample m.
\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{m,l}: its l-th noisy received realization.
\sigma^2: per-dimension AWGN variance.
The paper states that, using reparameterization and Monte-Carlo sampling, Equation (4) provides an unbiased gradient estimate for stochastic-gradient optimization.[arxiv]
Sparsity prior and KL approximation
For VFE, the paper selects a mean-field log-uniform variational prior:
q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
=
\prod_{i=1}^{n}q(\hat z_i),
\qquad
q(\log|\hat z_i|)=\mathrm{constant}.
Definitions:
n: encoded-feature dimension.
\hat z_i: i-th component of \hat{\mathbf{z}}.
The factorized prior is selected to encourage sparse feature dimensions.[arxiv]
The KL term is decomposed in Equation (5):
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\big(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})
\|q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\big)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\big(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat z_i\mid\mathbf{x})
\|q(\hat z_i)
\big).
\tag{5}
Because this KL term lacks a closed form for the selected prior, the paper uses the following approximation in Equation (6):
\begin{aligned}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\big(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat z_i\mid\mathbf{x})
\|q(\hat z_i)
\big)
&=
\frac{1}{2}\log\alpha_i
-
\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(1,\alpha_i)}
[\log|\epsilon|]
+
C
\\
&\approx
k_1 S(k_2+k_3\log\alpha_i)
-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\alpha_i^{-1})
+C,
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
with
\alpha_i=\frac{\sigma^2}{z_i^2},
\qquad
k_1=0.63576,\quad
k_2=1.87320,\quad
k_3=1.48695.
Definitions:
z_i: i-th transmitted feature/channel-symbol component.
\alpha_i: variance-to-squared-feature-value ratio used in this KL approximation.
S(\cdot): sigmoid function.
C: constant.
\epsilon in Equation (6): scalar random variable drawn from \mathcal{N}(1,\alpha_i); it is distinct from the channel-noise vector \boldsymbol{\epsilon}.
The paper states that the approximate KL is minimized as \alpha_i\rightarrow\infty, equivalently as z_i\rightarrow0, and this encourages sparsity.[arxiv]

Channel, encoder, and decoder
E. Channel model
What the paper explicitly states — Section II-A, p. 3
The communication channel is a scalar Gaussian channel, implemented during learning as a non-trainable layer:
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{z}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}.
The additive noise is:
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}
\sim
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^2\mathbf{I}).
The paper imposes a per-dimension peak-power constraint:
z_i^2\leq P,
\qquad
\forall i=1,\ldots,n.
Its channel-quality definition is peak SNR:
\mathrm{PSNR}
=
10\log\frac{P}{\sigma^2}
\quad \text{dB}.
Definitions:
\mathbf{z}: channel input/transmitted encoded feature.
\hat{\mathbf{z}}: channel output/noisy received feature.
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}: AWGN vector.
\sigma^2: AWGN variance.
P: permitted peak power per encoded-feature dimension.
n: dimension of the encoded feature.[arxiv]
Fading model: NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER. The paper uses additive Gaussian noise; it does not give a Rayleigh, Rician, Nakagami, Doppler, time-correlated, or UAV-mobility-induced fading equation.
SNR sampling during training: For static-channel VFE, the paper uses a specified fixed \sigma^2. For dynamic-channel VL-VFE, it treats \sigma^2 as a random variable sampled from a distribution p(\sigma^2), taken as uniform over a selected range for simplicity.[arxiv]
F. Encoder/representation
The on-device mapping is deterministic in the VIB development:
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})
=
\mathcal{N}
\big(
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
\mid
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\phi}),
\sigma^2\mathbf{I}
\big).
The paper says p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}) may be viewed as a Dirac-delta distribution because the on-device DNN itself is deterministic; randomness in \hat{\mathbf{z}} arises from the Gaussian channel.[arxiv]
For its prunable VFE encoder, the paper writes the fully connected layer as:
\mathrm{FC}(\mathbf{a})
=
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}
=
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}\widetilde{\mathbf{a}},
\tag{7}
where:
\mathbf{a}: FC-layer input.
\mathbf{W}: weight matrix.
\mathbf{b}: bias vector.
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=[\mathbf{W},\mathbf{b}]: augmented weight matrix.
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}=[\mathbf{a}^{T},1]^{T}: augmented input.
The VFE output component is Equation (8):
z_i
=
\mathrm{Tanh}
\left(
\gamma_i
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{i\cdot}}
{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{i\cdot}\|_2}
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
\right).
\tag{8}
Definitions:
z_i: i-th encoded/transmitted feature component.
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}): output of previous on-device layers.
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{i\cdot}: i-th row of the augmented weight matrix.
\gamma_i: learned importance/scale factor for output dimension i.
\|\cdot\|_2: Euclidean norm.
\mathrm{Tanh}: hyperbolic-tangent activation.
Since \mathrm{Tanh}(\cdot)\in[-1,1], the paper states that its VFE construction sets peak power P=1. A dimension is pruned if \gamma_i\leq\gamma_0, where \gamma_0 is a pruning threshold.[arxiv]
Continuous or discrete: The transmitted \mathbf{z} is continuous-valued in the proposed VFE/VL-VFE neural communication scheme. The paper’s proposed channel does not quantize \mathbf{z} before the AWGN layer.[arxiv]
Latent dimensionality: The maximum encoder dimension is denoted n. A universally fixed n is NOT SPECIFIED AS A GENERAL METHOD CONSTANT IN THE PAPER; it is an experimental/model-design choice. The released implementation examples use intermediate_dim=64 for shown MNIST and CIFAR VL-VFE commands, but that repository setting must not be treated as a universal equation-level requirement of the paper.[eprints.whiterose.ac]
G. Decoder/task model
The server-side DNN is the variational task estimator:
q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}).
Operationally, its relationship is:
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
\longmapsto
q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\longmapsto
\hat{\mathbf{y}}.
For the classification experiments, the negative log-likelihood in Equations (3)–(4) is the task loss associated with predicting the image label. The paper does not reconstruct raw \mathbf{x} at the receiver in its proposed task-oriented system.[arxiv]
Precise output decision rule, e.g. \hat y=\arg\max_c q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(y=c\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}): NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER’S mathematical formulation provided here. The paper specifies the conditional task-prediction distribution and negative log-likelihood, rather than a general hard-decision equation.
D/H. Task relevance and total loss
The paper’s central mathematical definition of “task-relevant” is information-theoretic, not simply accuracy-based:
\text{preserve task relevance}
\iff
\text{maximize } I(\hat Z,Y)
\iff
\text{minimize } H(Y\mid\hat Z).
The empirical implementation becomes a negative log-likelihood task term:
-\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}),
plus a compression/regularization term:
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x})
\|q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\right).
Thus the exact trainable total loss is Equation (3), estimated in Equation (4), not merely classification accuracy and not reconstruction MSE. In classification experiments, the first term is operationally a cross-entropy-style negative log-likelihood.[arxiv]
Interpretation: “Task relevance” in this paper means information in the channel-corrupted representation \hat Z that helps predict Y, under a simultaneous penalty on retaining information about X. It is not a general semantic-similarity definition and it is not a navigation-specific relevance definition.

Differentiability, training, evaluation
I. Differentiability and gradient path
What the paper explicitly states
The forward computation is:
\mathbf{x}
\rightarrow
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\phi})
\rightarrow
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{z}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}
\rightarrow
q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}).
The paper explicitly states that:
The device-side feature extractor and JSCC encoder are DNNs and can be jointly trained end-to-end.
The Gaussian channel is modeled as a non-trainable layer.
The method applies reparameterization and Monte-Carlo sampling to obtain an unbiased gradient estimate.
Parameters \boldsymbol{\phi} and \boldsymbol{\theta} are updated by backpropagation in Algorithms 1 and 2.[arxiv]
The resulting gradient path, as an implementation-level interpretation consistent with the stated additive model, is:
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VIB}}
\rightarrow
q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\rightarrow
\hat{\mathbf{z}}
\rightarrow
\mathbf{z}
\rightarrow
\boldsymbol{\phi}.
For the additive channel,
\hat{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{z}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon},
the sampled \boldsymbol{\epsilon} is treated as external random noise during the forward pass, while the additive mapping remains differentiable with respect to \mathbf{z}.
Non-differentiable operations:
During VFE pruning, the threshold decision \gamma_i\leq\gamma_0 is a discrete pruning/deactivation rule.
The paper’s core VIB loss is differentiable before this hard pruning decision.
The paper does not give a separate surrogate-gradient formula for the threshold comparison. Therefore, the exact gradient treatment of hard pruning/deactivation beyond the algorithmic description is NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER.[arxiv]
J. Training procedure
Static VFE — Algorithm 1, p. 6
Select minibatch \{(\mathbf{x}_m,\mathbf{y}_m)\}_{m=1}^{M}.
Compute \mathbf{z}_m using Equation (8).
Compute the KL approximation using Equation (6).
Sample L independent channel-noise vectors for each sample:
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{m,l}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^2\mathbf{I}).
Compute Equation (4).
Update \boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\theta} by backpropagation.
Prune dimension i if \gamma_i\leq\gamma_0.[arxiv]
Dynamic-channel VL-VFE — Section IV, pp. 6–8
The dynamic-channel objective is Equation (9):
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VIB}}(\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\mathbb{E}_{p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\sigma^2)}
\left[
\mathbb{E}_{p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x},\sigma^2)}
[-\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}})]
+
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x},\sigma^2)
\|q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\right)
\right].
\tag{9}
The sample-based estimate is Equation (10):
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VIB}}
\simeq
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\Bigg[
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}
-\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{y}_m\mid\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{m,l})
+
\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
p_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbf{x}_m,\sigma_m^2)
\|q(\hat{\mathbf{z}})
\right)
\Bigg],
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
where
\sigma_m^2\sim p(\sigma^2),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{m,l}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_m^2\mathbf{I}).
The paper says, for simplicity, it samples \sigma^2 uniformly from a chosen range to model dynamic channels.[arxiv]
The dynamic importance scale is Equation (11):
z_i
=
\mathrm{Tanh}
\left(
\gamma_i(\sigma^2)
\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{i\cdot}}
{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{i\cdot}\|_2}
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
\right).
\tag{11}
The consecutive-activation construction is Equation (12):
\gamma_i(\sigma^2)
=
\sum_{j=i}^{n}g_j(\sigma^2).
\tag{12}
Definitions:
\gamma_i(\sigma^2): channel-condition-dependent importance for feature dimension i.
g_j(\sigma^2): j-th output of an MLP \mathbf{g}(\cdot).
The paper constrains these functions so they are non-negative increasing functions.
This produces \gamma_i(\sigma^2)\geq\gamma_j(\sigma^2) for j>i, enabling consecutive active dimensions.
More dimensions are activated under worse channel conditions.[arxiv]
Optimizer, learning rate, epoch count, batch size, and exact L: NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER passages extracted above. The algorithms name T, M, L, \sigma^2, n, and \gamma_0 as inputs but do not make them universal constants. The public code repository identifies PyTorch 1.8.1 and Torchvision 0.9.1 and gives example beta and threshold values, but implementation-repository settings should be kept separate from claims about the original paper’s general mathematical method.[eprints.whiterose.ac]
K. Evaluation
What the paper explicitly evaluates — Section V, p. 8 onward
Tasks: image classification.
Datasets: MNIST and CIFAR-10; the appendix additionally tests Tiny ImageNet.
Primary task metric: classification/inference performance, i.e. classification accuracy.
Communication overhead: number of nonzero/activated feature dimensions; this is tied to latency and transmission overhead in the paper’s discussion.
Channel condition: PSNR, defined above.
Static condition: fixed channel noise variance.
Dynamic condition: variable noise variance/PSNR, using VL-VFE.
Baselines:
DeepJSCC trained with cross-entropy for classification rather than data reconstruction.
Learning-based quantization followed by assumed channel coding.
For the quantization baseline, the paper gives its assumed communication-rate expression as Equation (13):
C(P,\sigma^2)
=
\min
\left\{
\log_2
\left(
1+\sqrt{\frac{2P}{\pi e\sigma^2}}
\right),
\frac{1}{2}\log_2
\left(
1+\frac{P}{\sigma^2}
\right)
\right\}
\quad\text{b.p.c.u.}
\tag{13}
where C(P,\sigma^2) is assumed achievable rate in bits per channel use.[arxiv]
Fading-channel test procedure: NOT SPECIFIED IN THE PAPER. The evaluation is AWGN/PSNR based, not a UAV fading-channel experiment.

A UAV state-space definition, including a 6-dimensional state representation.
A continuous-time or discrete-time UAV dynamics model.
UAV kinematics.
Aerodynamic model.

Core distinction
The paper’s learned task target is a ground-truth driving-action-related output supplied by an expert/human-driving dataset. It does not optimize a simulator rollout cost such as position-to-goal distance, trajectory tracking of the actual physical vehicle state, Lyapunov stability, reward, or cost-to-go. Its central task loss is action/trajectory prediction conditioned on transmitted visual information, side information, and channel state.[arxiv][ar5iv.labs.arxiv]

PAPER-BACKED COMPONENTS WE CAN USE
A task-oriented communication architecture: f_e:\mathcal X\rightarrow\mathcal Z, Eq. (1), Sec. III, p. 4; a source input is encoded directly into complex JSCC symbols.
Average transmit-power constraint: \frac1{l_z}\sum_i|z_i|^2\le P_{\mathrm{target}}, Eq. (2), Sec. III, p. 4.
Noisy fading-channel simulation abstraction: \check{\mathbf z}=\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf z+\mathbf n, Eq. (4), Sec. III, p. 4, with complex Gaussian noise and frequency-selective OFDM context.
Perfect-CSI equalization baseline: \hat{\mathbf z}=\frac{h^*}{|h|^2}\check{\mathbf z}, Eq. (5), Sec. III, p. 4, if your simulation deliberately assumes perfect CSI.
Conditional task receiver: f_a(\hat{\mathbf z},\mathbf m,\mathbf h)\mapsto\hat{\mathbf a}, Eq. (6), Sec. III, pp. 4–5. This supports conditioning a task network on received representation plus side/channel information.
Bandwidth/channel-use accounting: compression-ratio constraint Eq. (8), p. 5; information-rate constraint Eq. (10), p. 5; power constraint Eq. (2), p. 4.
Task-oriented VIB principle: \mathcal L_{\mathrm{IB}}, Eq. (11), p. 5, and its tractable VIB upper bound \mathcal L_{\mathrm{VIB}}, Eq. (17), p. 6.
Mini-batch VIB training estimator: Eq. (18), Sec. IV, p. 6.
Delay decomposition: \delta=\delta_e+\delta_u+\delta_a+\delta_d+\delta_c, Sec. V-A, pp. 6–7.
Variable-length/energy-based JSCC-symbol selection concept: Eq. (19), Sec. V, p. 6, subject to accounting for index overhead.
COMPONENTS THAT CAN TRANSFER ONLY AFTER MODIFICATION
Source representation: replace Diao’s visual \mathbf x_t with your selected UAV source. A six-dimensional state can be used, but that substitution is your design choice, not the paper’s.
Task variable A: replace driving action labels with a UAV-relevant supervised target only after you formally define it.
Conditional side information M: decide whether UAV velocity, goal, time, link estimate, or other variables should be in the transmitted source versus reliably conveyed side information.
DTCP delay idea: use its delay ledger and prediction-horizon concept only after defining UAV actuation delay, manoeuvres, and a valid prediction target.
Resource comparison: use equal l_z/channel-use and power budgets across digital, reconstruction-oriented DeepJSCC, and task-oriented DeepJSCC; Diao provides the constraint language, but does not specify your benchmark protocol.
Channel model: OFDM fading can be retained if it matches your simulation assumptions; otherwise another fading model requires independent justification.
Joint VIB training: conceptually transferable, but the decoder/task architecture, labels, channel distribution, and concrete loss must be independently specified.

Answer to the central question: Diao et al. genuinely provide the mathematical communication chain f_e\rightarrow noisy fading channel \rightarrow equalization \rightarrow conditional task agent; a power/compression/rate framework; a conditional VIB loss that ties received latent symbols to action-relevant information; and an explicit E2E delay decomposition with delay-aware command prediction. These are useful foundations for the communication and edge task-inference portions of a closed-loop Task-Oriented DeepJSCC UAV simulation. They leave unspecified the essential mathematics required to complete a closed-loop UAV navigation system: UAV plant dynamics, state/control constraints, UAV controller, goal-reaching objective, control/trajectory cost, simulator rollout loss, gradient flow through dynamics, exact resource-allocation policy, and the detailed numerical training/evaluation configuration needed for your proposed benchmark.[arxiv][ar5iv.labs.arxiv]
