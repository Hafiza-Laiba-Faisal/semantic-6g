# Part 1 — Main Results: M1 and M2

<!-- pagebreak -->
## Figure 1 — M1 Success Rate vs SNR (k = 1, 2, 3)

![Figure 1 — M1 success rate vs SNR for k = 1, 2, 3.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig1_m1_sr_vs_snr.png)

**What it shows.** M1 evaluates the severe-bandwidth regime at k = 1, 2, and 3. The panel plots goal-reaching success rate against SNR for the reconstruction-oriented and task-oriented DeepJSCC arms, on both AWGN and Rayleigh fading. The reconstruction arm learns a usable representation and reaches roughly 24–31% success; the frozen task arm stays at 2–5% and is almost flat across SNR.

**What to notice.**
- The task arm is not merely worse than reconstruction; it is almost unresponsive to SNR. That points to an optimisation/objective failure rather than a channel limitation.
- Reconstruction is the stronger neural baseline in this regime.
- This is the observation that motivated the Wave-A diagnosis in Chapter 5.3.

**Best placement.** Chapter 5.1 (M1 results); optionally Chapter 4 as the first baseline.

**File.** `fig1_m1_sr_vs_snr.png`

<!-- pagebreak -->
## Figure 2 — M2 Three-Way Comparison on AWGN

![Figure 2 — M2 three-way comparison on AWGN, k = 12 to 54.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig2_m2_awgn.png)

**What it shows.** M2 is the main matched-budget comparison at k = 12, 18, 24, 30, and 54 on AWGN. Each panel shows success rate versus SNR for the reconstruction, task, and digital methods. The digital budget is matched exactly through the frozen (k, B) pairs, which satisfy 6B + 6 = k.

**What to notice.**
- At k = 12 and 18, reconstruction is the best method; the digital link is payload-limited.
- At k = 24 the digital link overtakes; at k = 30 and 54 it dominates.
- The frozen task arm remains low at every k, so the crossover is between reconstruction and digital, not the task arm.
- This is the strongest single figure for the thesis; use it first when presenting the main result.

**Best placement.** Chapter 5.2 (M2 main result).

**File.** `fig2_m2_awgn.png`

## Figure 3 — M2 Three-Way Comparison on Rayleigh Fading

![Figure 3 — M2 three-way comparison on Rayleigh fading.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig3_m2_rayleigh.png)

**What it shows.** The same comparison as Figure 2, but on slow Rayleigh fading. The qualitative ordering is preserved: reconstruction wins at low k, digital wins at high k, and the digital link loses more at low SNR because of fading.

**What to notice.**
- The crossover survives fading.
- Digital at k = 54 still reaches near 1.0 at high SNR; reconstruction stays near 0.25.
- The task arm is again flat and low.
- Use this next to Figure 2 to show that the result is not an AWGN-only artefact.

**Best placement.** Chapter 5.2 (channel robustness).

**File.** `fig3_m2_rayleigh.png`

# Part 4 — Wave-A and the Mechanism

## Figure 20 — Wave-A Arm Comparison

![Figure 20 — Wave-A arm comparison at k = 1 and k = 12.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig_wave_a_arms.png)

**What it shows.** Wave-A arm comparison at k = 1 and k = 12, on AWGN and Rayleigh. The arms are the frozen task loss, λ-light (0.01), λ-heavy (0.1), capacity 256, fixed-SNR training, longer training, reconstruction auxiliary, and the seed-variance control.

**What to notice.**
- Only λ-heavy reaches about 1.0 on both channels at k = 12.
- λ-light works on AWGN (0.995) but collapses on Rayleigh (0.019), so the strength of the shaping matters.
- Capacity 256, fixed-SNR training, longer training, and the reconstruction auxiliary do not fix the task arm.
- The seed-variance control stays low, so the metric is not trivially exploitable.

**Best placement.** Chapter 5.3 (Wave-A diagnosis).

## Figure 21 — Gradient-Conditioning Mechanism

![Figure 21 — Encoder gradient norm and per-step distribution.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig_training_stability.png)

**What it shows.** Gradient-conditioning mechanism. Left: encoder gradient norm over training for 18 runs on a log scale. Right: per-step gradient distribution with 5–95% whiskers. Frozen and A-12 runs reach roughly 10¹⁴–10¹⁷; λ-heavy runs stay at O(1).

## Figure 35 — M3 Digital Infeasibility Boundary

![Figure 35 — M3: the matched-budget digital baseline cannot exist at k = 1, 2, 3.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig_m3_infeasibility.png)

**What it shows.** M3 is a structural display, not a measured performance result. At k = 1, 2, and 3 the smallest matched-budget digital packet would need 6B + 6 = 12 channel uses at B = 1, while only 1, 2, or 3 uses are available. The deficits are therefore 11, 10, and 9 channel uses. Panel (b) places the three M3 cells beside the five measured M2 points and shows the first feasible packet at k = 12 (B = 1, 6B + 6 = 12).

**What to notice.**
- The digital baseline is **infeasible by construction** at k = 1–3; it is not missing because it was forgotten or because it lost.
- This is why M1 has only the reconstruction and task arms: there is no matched-budget digital cell to run at those bandwidths.
- The neural arms have no equivalent packet constraint, so they can operate at k = 1–3. That is a structural property of the comparison, not a coding gain.
- M3 is a feasibility boundary, not an empirical result. Never quote it as a success rate.

**Best placement.** Chapter 5.2.1 (M3); also Chapter 4.5.1 for the design explanation.

## Figure 37 — 3GPP UMa-Aerial Channel Model

![Figure 37 — 3GPP TR 36.777 / TR 38.901 UMa-aerial model used in A-10, A-12, and A-13.](fyp_md_files/figures/fig_3gpp_channel_model.png)

**What it shows.** The 3GPP UMa-aerial model is a first-class channel model in this thesis, not only an appendix note. Panel (a) defines the geometry: a UAV at height h = 50 m and ground distance d₂D from the ground station, with 3-D distance d₃D. Panel (b) plots the TR 36.777 LoS probability against ground distance. Panel (c) plots the LoS, NLoS, and mean path loss at f_c = 2 GHz. At the pinned geometry d₂D = 500 m, the model gives P_LoS = 0.889, PL_LoS = 93.45 dB, PL_NLoS = 113.09 dB, and PL_mean = 95.63 dB.

**What to notice.**
- A-10 uses the large-scale layer: a deterministic gain shifts the effective SNR, and the LoS/NLoS curves are mixed with P_LoS.
- A-12 and A-13 add the per-episode dispersion: one LoS/NLoS draw per episode, TR 38.901 shadowing (4 dB LoS, 6 dB NLoS), and block fading over the episode.
- The model does **not** include Doppler, time-correlated shadowing, altitude-dependent small-scale fading, antenna patterns, noise figure, or bandwidth. Those are stated limits, not hidden assumptions.
- The mean path loss of about 95.6 dB is why the A-10 transmit-SNR numbers are in the 87–108 dB range; they are relative to a unit-gain benchmark, not a hardware budget.

**Best placement.** Chapter 3.8 (system model); refer back from Chapter 5.10 (A-10/A-12/A-13).
