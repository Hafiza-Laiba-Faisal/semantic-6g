*1. "UAV khud ko message send kar rahi hai?" - Architecture clarification*

Our implemented system is *UAV-to-ground-station (uplink)*, not UAV-to-self. The UAV is the *transmitter* of its 6-D state [x, y, vx, vy, xg, yg], and the *ground station* is a separate receiver node that decodes a task-relevant representation and computes the control, which is applied back to the UAV. This is the standard C&C scenario where navigation computation is offloaded from a compute-constrained drone to the ground segment - the same link direction as the UAV command-and-control literature (proposal Section 14). The original topic statement described the symmetric downlink; the implemented, defensible scope is the state-report uplink, the bandwidth-critical direction of that same C&C loop. *One sentence will be added* to the system model: "the return command downlink is assumed ideal and out of scope; the wireless bottleneck studied is the uplink state transmission."

*2. Research Question wording*

You are right: RQ1 ("to what extent does task-oriented outperform...") presumes the direction of the result, and contradicts proposal Section 22 ("no direction presumed in advance"). We request replacing RQ1 with the neutral, conditional form:

*"RQ1 (revised): Under what conditions - channel type (AWGN vs Rayleigh), SNR, and bandwidth ratio - does task-oriented DeepJSCC trained directly on navigation performance outperform, match, or underperform conventional digital communication and reconstruction-oriented DeepJSCC for UAV state transmission?"*

This matters scientifically: our completed experiments (150/150 cells, 3 seeds, 5,000 paired test episodes per unit, McNemar + Wilson CI statistics) show the answer is *regime-dependent* - reconstruction beats task-oriented training at low bandwidth, and digital wins decisively at sufficient bandwidth. The neutral RQ turns this from a "failed confirmation" into the project's central finding: a characterized regime boundary with root-cause diagnosis.

*3. Novelty*

Our defensible, evidence-backed claims are:
(1) Domain transfer + closed-loop extension: Diao et al.'s task-oriented JSCC-for-control is image-based autonomous driving; we transfer it to a low-dimensional UAV kinematic state AND backpropagate end-to-end through closed-loop UAV dynamics, which the source literature does not specify for UAVs (documented in our audit).
(2) First systematic matched-bandwidth three-way comparison (digital vs reconstruction vs task-oriented) for UAV navigation under both AWGN and Rayleigh fading - not present in the reviewed literature.
(3) Regime-mapping: exactly when task-orientation helps vs hurts as a function of bandwidth/SNR, with root-cause diagnosis.
(4) A reproducible frozen-protocol testbed.

We do *not* claim a new coding paradigm; our claim is deliberately modest and matches the evidence.
