2.5 State normalization

Neural networks ke liye raw meters/velocity directly use nahi karenge.

State:

$$ s_t=[x,y,v_x,v_y,x_g,y_g]. $$

Positions ko known simulation range se normalize karenge.

For position:

$$ \boxed{ x_{\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{x}{8} } $$

and similarly \(y,x_g,y_g\).

Velocity:

$$ \boxed{ v_{x,\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{v_x}{v_{\max}}, \qquad v_{y,\mathrm{norm}}=\frac{v_y}{v_{\max}}. } $$

Therefore approximately:

$$ s_t^{norm}\in[-1,1]^6. $$

This is a neural-network conditioning choice, not a claim that the DeepJSCC papers mandate this exact normalization.

2.7 Task loss weights

Start simple.

Rather than introducing many arbitrary weights, primary loss:

$$ \boxed{ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}} = \frac1T \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|p_t-p_g\|_2^2 + \lambda_T \|p_T-p_g\|_2^2. } $$

Use:

$$ \boxed{\lambda_T=2.0} $$

initially.

Why? Final position should matter somewhat more than an intermediate position.

But this is a project-design hyperparameter and must be validated by ablation.

We don't want to introduce velocity/control penalties immediately because they can make the interpretation of the communication objective unnecessarily complicated.

2.8 Reconstruction loss

For Reconstruction DeepJSCC:

$$ \boxed{ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}} = \frac1{6} \|s_t-\hat s_t\|_2^2. } $$

Because components are normalized first, position and velocity dimensions don't dominate merely because of their units.

2.13 Conventional digital baseline

This needs particular care.

Pipeline:

$$ \boxed{ s \rightarrow \text{normalize} \rightarrow \text{quantize} \rightarrow \text{channel coding} \rightarrow \text{QPSK} \rightarrow \text{channel} \rightarrow \text{demodulation} \rightarrow \text{channel decoding} \rightarrow \text{dequantization} } $$
Quantization

Initially:

$$ \boxed{8\text{ bits/component}} $$

for the six state components.

So:

$$ 6\times8=48\text{ source bits/state}. $$

However, we cannot simply throw these 48 bits into the same \(k=1,2,3\) complex-symbol budget without defining the digital rate/code/modulation relationship.

This is exactly why the digital baseline must be specified carefully before coding.

2.14 Digital channel coding

Recommended first implementation:

$$ \boxed{\text{rate-}1/2\text{ convolutional code}} $$

with a standard generator configuration.

But I would not freeze this as final yet.

Reason: the code rate changes the number of coded bits, which directly changes how many QPSK symbols are needed. Therefore it affects the fairness comparison.

We need to solve:

$$ \boxed{ \text{source bits} \rightarrow \text{coded bits} \rightarrow \text{modulation symbols} \rightarrow \text{available channel uses} } $$

under the same bandwidth budget.

2.15 Digital baseline fairness

This is the biggest thing we must settle before coding.

For DeepJSCC:

$$ \rho=\frac{6}{k} $$

in the proposal's source/channel-use convention.

For the digital system, the number of available bits is determined by:

$$ N_{\mathrm{bits}} = 2kR_c $$

for QPSK, because QPSK carries 2 coded bits/symbol.

Thus with \(R_c=1/2\):

$$ N_{\mathrm{bits}}=k. $$

For \(k=1,2,3\), this gives only:

$$ 1,\;2,\;3 $$

information bits per state.

That is clearly incompatible with our naive 48-bit quantized state.

Therefore:

Do NOT code the digital baseline yet.

We need one more explicit fairness decision:

How exactly are source bits, quantization precision, FEC rate, modulation order, and channel-use budget matched?

Otherwise the digital baseline can become artificially weak or artificially strong.
