# M1 Analysis — Task-Oriented vs Reconstruction-Oriented DeepJSCC (Low-k Regime)

**Date:** 2026-10-06 · **Scope:** analysis & reporting only — **no re-training, no re-evaluation**; M1 is complete and every number below is read from stored artifacts (`results/M1/step10_m1_summary.json` + 198 paired eval unit JSONs, themselves untouched).
**Companion artifact:** `results/M1/m1_analysis_tables.json` (new derivation file; no historical JSON modified).

---

## 1. Data provenance (why these numbers are trustworthy)

- 36/36 neural training runs complete (task + reconstruction × k ∈ {1,2,3} × AWGN/Rayleigh × seeds {42,43,44}), 0 failed, 0 NaN, frozen 9C protocol.
- 198/198 paired evaluation units: 66 conditions (3 k × 2 channels × 11 SNRs {0,2,…,20} dB) × 3 seeds; **5000 paired test episodes per unit per method** (test seed 10042).
- Paired by construction: both methods see **bitwise-identical** episodes, noise and fading realizations; identical 6-D state, controller (u = clip(−Kp(p−p_g) − Kv·v, ±2)), goal radius 0.5 m, horizon T = 100.
- Pooled tables below aggregate **15,000 episodes per condition per method** (3 seeds × 5000).

## 2. Headline finding (carefully stated)

**Measured fact:** at every one of the 198 paired cells — all k ∈ {1,2,3}, both channels, all 11 SNRs, all 3 seeds — the reconstruction-oriented DeepJSCC reached the goal more often than the task-oriented variant (McNemar n10 > n01 in 198/198), had smaller final/average distance, and used less control effort.

**What this is NOT:** it is not a claim that task-oriented DeepJSCC is inherently inferior. This is the **low-bandwidth regime only** (ρ = k/6 ≤ 1/2). M1's own design anticipates that the ranking may change at the larger matched budgets of M2 (k ∈ {12,…,54}, ρ up to 9); no superiority is presumed for any method there.

**Reading the tables:**
- Reconstruction is 5–10× the task-oriented success rate everywhere (e.g., AWGN k=2: 0.309 vs 0.033 at 4 dB).
- **SNR-threshold note:** even the best low-k configuration never reaches SR = 0.50 — neither method "solves" navigation at ρ ≤ 1/2; M4's near-lossless B=8 reference (54 uses) achieves SR ≈ 0.97–1.00, so the ceiling is bandwidth, not the task.
- **Measured non-monotonicity (worth flagging, not over-interpreting):** reconstruction's SR peaks around 2–6 dB and gently declines toward 20 dB (0.309 → 0.242 at k=2 AWGN), while its final-distance and control-effort improve monotonically with SNR (3.51 → 3.38 m; 704 → 669). Task-oriented curves are essentially flat in SNR (e.g., k=2 AWGN: 0.035 → 0.027; min-distance stays ≈ 6.4–6.6 m — the UAV never closes on the goal). Possible explanations (marked **interpretation, not established**): (a) the decoders were trained on SNR ~ U(0,20) and their error structure at the high-SNR edge differs (bias-dominated vs noise-like error, which the closed loop may tolerate differently); (b) boundary-exit semantics interact with reconstruction quality (see §6). A dedicated diagnostic would be needed to settle it; none is planned before M2.

## 4. Significance audit (corrected McNemar + paired-t)

**McNemar correction (Step-10E):** the stored p-values implement `p = 0.5·erfc(√(χ²/2))` — a one-sided tail, exactly half the two-sided χ²(1) survival function. The fix (commit `88e107a`) is `p = erfc(√(χ²/2))`, i.e. **corrected p = 2 × stored p, exactly; χ² values are unaffected**. All 198 stored JSONs still carry the stale (halved) values — they are historical artifacts and were not modified; regeneration is a tracked action item pending supervisor approval. **All conclusions below use corrected p = 2 × stored.**

- Reconstruction wins (n10 > n01): **198/198** cells. Discordance ranges: n01 (task better) 0–387, n10 (recon better) 471–1563 of 5000 pairs.
- Corrected p < 0.05: **198/198**. Largest corrected p = **1.04 × 10⁻²⁶** (k1 Rayleigh, seed 43, 0 dB) — every cell is overwhelmingly significant.
- **Per-seed agreement:** reconstruction beats task in all 3 seeds independently in **66/66 conditions** — the finding is not seed-driven.
- **Paired-t** (task − reconstruction, 5000 paired episodes per cell):
  - final distance: task worse in 198/198; significant 198/198; t ∈ [25.6, 134.5]
  - average distance: task worse in 198/198; significant 198/198; t ∈ [26.8, 134.6]
  - control effort: task **higher** in 198/198 (task also spends more energy while performing worse); significant 198/198; t ∈ [7.8, 88.5]

Suggested thesis wording: *"Across all 198 paired cells (66 conditions × 3 seeds), the reconstruction-oriented variant outperformed the task-oriented variant on success rate (McNemar, corrected two-sided p < 0.05 in every cell; largest 1.0×10⁻²⁶), final and average distance, and control effort (paired-t, p < 0.05 in every cell), with unanimous per-seed agreement."*

## 6. MSE metric — post-exit limitation (report MSE as diagnostic only)

`aggregate.mse_mean` is the trajectory mean of ‖ŝ̄−s̄‖²/6 over t = 0…T−1. The closed-loop rollout **terminates an episode when the UAV exits the workspace boundary**, and after termination the harness feeds the true state through (ŝ = s), so **exited episodes contribute exactly zero MSE for the remainder of the horizon**. Consequences, quantified by the Step-10E matched-window diagnostic (`diagnostics/matched_mse_diagnostic.py`, 500 episodes, task k=1 AWGN):

- At 0 dB, **100% of task episodes exited** → MSE_full = 0.378 is a mixture of real reconstruction error (active phase) and artificial zeros (post-exit suffix); MSE over "active-only" episodes is undefined here (0 non-exited episodes).
- MSE_full (0.378) matches the stored aggregate mse_mean (0.375) — the definitions agree; it is the *definition itself* that is censored by exits.
- The **single-step initial MSE** (same definition as the training-time `recon_mse_diag`) is ≈ 62 — a ratio of ≈ **164×** vs the trajectory MSE. This fully explains why training logs show large recon-MSE diagnostics while evaluation tables show small mse_mean: different windows (single step at t=0 vs trajectory average), different SNR distributions (fixed vs U(0,20)), plus the post-exit zeroing.

**Reporting rule for the thesis:** MSE columns are **not comparable across methods or SNRs** when exit rates differ (methods with more exits get more zero-suffix). Success rate (with paired McNemar) is the primary metric; distances and control effort are the honest continuous secondary metrics; treat MSE as a diagnostic with this caveat stated.

## 7. Limitations of the M1 finding

1. **Low-k regime only** (ρ ≤ 1/2): the reconstruction advantage is measured at k ∈ {1,2,3}; M2's k ∈ {12,…,54} is the open empirical question — no extrapolation in either direction.
2. **3 training seeds** (42/43/44) with unanimity across seeds, but absolute SRs at ρ ≤ 1/2 are far from the near-lossless reference (M4), so both methods are in a heavily bandwidth-starved regime where training variance is large (documented: per-run task-loss reduction ranged 4%–94% across seeds under the same frozen protocol).
3. **Success semantics:** success = ∃t ≤ T with d ≤ 0.5 m; boundary exit terminates the episode. Exit-rate differences across methods/SNRs also censor MSE (§6).
4. **Non-monotonic SR in SNR** (§3) is measured but not yet causally explained; flagged for future diagnostic work, no claim made.
5. Stored McNemar p-values in historical JSONs remain stale (factor 2); use corrected values (2 × stored) until regeneration is approved.
